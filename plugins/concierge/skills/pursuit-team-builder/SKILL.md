# Pursuit Team builder — "who should work this deal, and set it up"

Pull together the **pursuit team** for **$ARGUMENTS** — an opportunity (an ID or name), a document the user shared
(an RFP or brief), or a client call — and lay it out as a board: the pursuit team, the **client**, the **people
worth knowing about** and the **relevant engagement experience**, ready for the account team to work on.

**A pursuit team works the deal, not the engagement.** It builds the estimate, joins the sales calls, sets the
technical direction and writes the proposal. Some of them may deliver the work later, but choosing who delivers is
staffing - offer it at the end, don't do it here. Its roles:
- **Pursuit lead** - owns the pursuit and the client conversation: senior, client-facing, ideally someone the client
  already knows.
- **Solution architect** - sets the technical direction: the deepest delivered experience across the opportunity's core
  skills, in architect or lead roles.
- **Estimator** - builds the estimate: has led or managed comparable engagements (engagement manager, project lead),
  so knows what the work really takes.
- **Subject-matter experts** - one or two, for skill areas or industry knowledge the others don't cover: they join the
  calls and write their parts of the proposal.

Three to five people in all; one person can hold two pursuit roles (say so). **A call** ("who should be on the
call?"): the 2–3 of them best placed for that conversation, plus anyone already on the pursuit.

If it's unclear which opportunity, ask. If no opportunity is given, offer the open opportunities closing soonest
(`list_opportunities` with `fields: "Basic"`); if a name is given, find its ID in that list, and if several match,
ask. A shared RFP or brief with no opportunity in the system works too: take the scope, skills, dates and client from
the document.

## How to write

Everything the user sees, including the short updates between steps, is written for a business reader such as a
seller, delivery lead or executive.

- **Plain business language.** Name people, clients, engagements, opportunities and skills by name. Never show
  database keys, internal IDs, field or column names, tool names or parameters (no "skill key 54", "yearsExperience",
  "EngagementID", "list_clients"). Write "years of experience", "hours on delivered work", "win probability". An ID
  the user typed themselves (e.g. an opportunity ID) can be echoed back once.
- **Linked names.** When the Concierge server gives a link format (in its instructions or at the end of a tool result), write each person, client,
  engagement, opportunity and technical skill name as a link to it in Concierge (first mention per section). A board,
  lineup or evaluation is linked with the `link` its tool result gives, as it is - never an address you build. Leave
  links out of anything written for a client.
- **No guessed pronouns.** Refer to people by name or "they"; never infer pronouns from a name ("Why them", not
  "Why her").
- **Succinct.** Lead with the answer. Prefer a short table or tight bullets to paragraphs. Skip preamble and closing
  recaps, and don't narrate how you got the data ("the payload was large", "I extracted…"). An update between steps
  is one short line, such as "Found 42 people with business intelligence experience; checking who's free next week."
- **Evidence-based.** Every claim about a person, the firm or a deal rests on the data. Say where it comes from in
  plain terms ("1,400 hours across 5 engagements").
- **Two kinds of skill evidence; know which you're citing.**
  - **Delivered:** hours on engagements, roles held, certifications and training. This is Concierge's
    differentiator; lead with it and rank on it.
  - **Self-reported:** what the person lists on their profile, including years of experience. It's fair to cite;
    say it's self-reported (e.g. "8 years of experience, self-reported").
  - A skill with only self-reported backing shows as Unverified. Say so plainly; it isn't zero ability.
- **Gaps in a sentence.** When data is missing or thin, say what's missing and what you did instead, in one or two
  plain sentences. Don't speculate about the database.

## Working style — use what this client offers

Use the host's built-in tools when they exist, and fall back to plain text when they don't (this skill also runs in
clients without them):
- **Choices** — when the user has to pick (which opportunity, which client, which option), use the AskUserQuestion tool
  if available: short labels, one-line descriptions. Otherwise list numbered options in text. Ask several questions
  together only when each stands on its own: one that matters only for some answers to another waits - ask the
  deciding question alone, then the follow-up only if the answer calls for it.
- **Progress** — for work with several rounds of calls, keep a live task list with the TodoWrite tool if available
  (one item per round, marked in progress and done as you go). Otherwise skip it — don't narrate.
- **Collateral** — when the output is something to share or keep (a brief, a proposal, bios), offer to save it as a
  document: a Word (.docx) or PowerPoint (.pptx) file or an artifact if the client can create them, otherwise a
  markdown file. Always show the content inline too.
- **Timelines and charts** — when the content is a schedule or a trend (a proposal timeline, who frees up when, a
  forecast by month), render it as a timeline or chart (an artifact or visual) if the client supports one; otherwise a
  table.
- **Next steps** — end by offering 2–3 relevant next actions, as a choice (AskUserQuestion) when available.

## Getting the data — fewest, smallest calls

- **Batch, never loop.** For several people, one call covers them all: `list_employees` (`ids` plus `fields`),
  `get_employees_technical_skills`, `get_employees_dir_scores`, `get_employees_availability_for_period`,
  `get_employees_financial_data`. Never call
  `get_employee_by_id` or another single-person tool once per person when a batch tool covers it.
- **Parallel by default.** Calls that don't need each other's results go out together, in one round. "In parallel"
  below means one message with all of those calls.
- **Only what you'll use.** Pass `fields` (`list_employees`, `list_engagements_by_status`, `list_opportunities`),
  `technicalSkillKeys` on `get_employees_technical_skills` (several people's skill evidence in one call), and `includeEngagements: false` on `list_clients`. For one
  opportunity, use `get_opportunity_by_id` (it includes roles and required skills) rather than listing them all.
- **Shortlist compactly.** `list_employees_with_technical_skills_by_skill_keys` always with `compact: true` and a
  `limit` (20 unless a step says otherwise): strongest match first, a fraction of the size.
- **Once per run.** Fetch reference lists (`list_technical_skills`, `list_clients`) once; reuse earlier results and
  filter lists yourself rather than calling again.
- **Cap the deep dives.** Tools with no batch version (`get_engagement_by_id`,
  `get_employee_engagement_role_assignments`) are only for the shortlist the steps name (8 at most unless a step says
  otherwise), after narrowing with list calls, all in one parallel round.

**Reading DIR.** `get_employees_dir_scores` returns `CompleteScore` (use this as the person's DIR) and
`OperationalScore`. DIR runs from -5 to +5, measured against replacement level (0), so it is not a 0–10 score. The
bands are the ones the Concierge app uses:
- 2.0 and up: Exceptional
- 0.25 to 2.0: Above Average
- -0.5 to 0.25: Average
- -1.5 to -0.5: Below Average
- under -1.5: Needs Improvement

Show the score to two decimals with its band, for example "1.31 (Above Average)". Ignore `CalculationTimestamp`.

## Data gathering

**Round 1, in parallel:**
1. `get_opportunity_by_id`: the client, stage, value, close date, **POV**, its roles' required skills and
   `pursuitTeam` (the people already working the deal - may be empty; ids starting EMP are employees, the rest sales
   staff). From a document instead: its scope, skills and dates.
2. `list_technical_skills`: to turn the required skill names into skill keys.
3. `list_clients` with `includeEngagements: false`: client industries (and the client's ID, from a document).

**Round 2, in parallel:**
4. `get_client_by_id` for the opportunity's client: its industry and engagements with the firm. If there's no client
   record, it's a new logo: no client card, and no relationship people or client engagements.
5. `list_employees_with_technical_skills_by_skill_keys` with all the opportunity's skill keys, `deliveryOnly: true`,
   `compact: true`, `limit: 20`: the people who've delivered this work.

**Round 3, in parallel:**
- `get_engagement_by_id` for the client's 4 most recent engagements: their teams - the people who've worked for this
  client, and who led them.
- The checks below, for the strongest 10 holders (highest skill scores across the opportunity's skills), the client's
  recent engagement leads and the pursuit team's employees - at most 15 people in all:
6. `get_employees_technical_skills` with their `ids` and `technicalSkillKeys` set to the opportunity's skills (one
   call): the engagements behind each person's skills - and the roles they held on them (architect, lead, manager).
7. `list_employees` with the same `ids` and `fields: "Basic,SoftSkills"` (one call): their titles, and their
   communication and presentation scores.
8. `get_employees_dir_scores`: their **DIR** - their delivery record, which is their credibility in front of the client.
9. `get_employees_availability_for_period` from today to the close date (the next 4 weeks if it's past or missing - the
   pursuit happens now, not during the engagement): their **CSOT**. Pursuit work is part-time, so look for someone
   who isn't overbooked (CSOT under ~100%), not for a free calendar.

**Choose** the pursuit roles above, in this order:
- **Pursuit lead:** an existing pursuit-team member in that role if there is one; else the most senior client-facing
  person with a relationship to this client (round 3), then strong communication and presentation, then DIR.
- **Solution architect:** delivered evidence across the most of the opportunity's skills, in architect or lead roles,
  then DIR.
- **Estimator:** led or managed the most comparable engagements (the same skills or industry), then DIR.
- **Subject-matter experts:** for important skills or industry knowledge the three above don't cover, delivered
  evidence first.
- For everyone: someone overbooked in the pursuit window (CSOT over ~100%) only if no one else fits - say so.
- For a call: the 2–3 best for that conversation - client-facing ability and the topic's evidence first.

**Round 4: comparable work.** From the evidence in step 6, plus `list_engagements_by_status` with
`statuses: ["COMPLETED", "ACTIVE"]`, `fields: "Basic"` (each engagement's `RequiredSkills`, `ClientIndustry` and
`ClientSatisfactionScore`), pick up to 6 delivered engagements in the technology and/or this industry: exact skills
first, then the same industry, then most recent - preferring ones delivered well (client satisfaction 4 or above).
These are the proof points for the calls and the proposal.

**Relevant people**, two kinds (a few of each, people not on the pursuit team):
- **Relationship:** people who've worked for this client before (the client's engagement teams, round 3).
- **Technology / industry:** people who've delivered engagements in the opportunity's skills and/or this industry -
  worth a call while shaping the solution (the runners-up and the evidence).

## The board

Its groups, in this order (leave out a group with nothing in it; a person or engagement appears once, in the first
group that fits), every card with a short `note` on why it is there:
1. **Opportunity Details**: the opportunity, then the client.
2. **Recommended Pursuit Team** (or **Recommended Call Team**, for a call): first the existing pursuit team's
   employees (note their pursuit role, e.g. "On the pursuit: Estimate Owner"; sales staff aren't cards - name them in
   the reply), then your picks (note: their pursuit role and why, e.g. "Solution architect - led Snowflake design on 4
   engagements").
3. **Relevant People**: the relationship people (note: "Worked with OhioHealth on Strategy Development for Business
   Intelligence, 2023"), then the technology / industry people (note: what they delivered).
4. **Relevant Engagements**: the client's engagements with us (most recent first, at most 6), then the comparable
   delivered work (note: why it's relevant, and how it went).

Call `create_board` with:
- **title:** "[Client] - [Opportunity]", e.g. "OhioHealth - Warehouse Automation" (the opportunity alone for a new
  logo; the client and topic for a call with no opportunity)
- **groups:** as above.
- **summary**: markdown for the board's first message, for the account team: one line on the opportunity (stage,
  value, close date, POV), then a short table (Pursuit role | Person | Why, from the evidence | CSOT), then one line
  on the comparable work. Plain business language: names, not IDs. If it started from a document the user shared (an
  RFP, a brief), name it there - the board can't hold the file itself.

If `create_board` isn't available, or it returns an error, still give the user the team, relevant people and
engagements below, and say the board couldn't be created and why, in one plain sentence.

## Reply

Internal: DIR, CSOT and rates stay inside the firm.

Offer next steps: `/concierge:pursuit-qualifier` for the go/no-go, `/concierge:evidence-bios` for the team's

**Lineups on the staffing board.** An opportunity may already have delivery teams built and evaluated in Concierge.

lineup and an `evaluationLink` to its evaluation. When the work is about an opportunity's team, check it once, and:
- **Use the evaluated team:** the highest-scoring completed evaluation's lineup is the team the firm has settled on; say

- **Link, don't build:** use the links exactly as returned. Never build a lineup or evaluation address yourself.
- **None yet:** say so in a line, and offer staffing it in Concierge.
